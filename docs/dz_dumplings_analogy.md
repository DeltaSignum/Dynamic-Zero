## Dumplings Analogy — Coupled Pneumatic Mass

  ---

  ![Dumplings analogy](images/dumplings_analogy.jpg)

  ---

The accordion is a conceptual analogy for the coupled pneumatic mass in DZ. It should be understood as one continuous structure rather than as several independent masses.

Its individual sections represent successive inertial elements. Each section receives the state inherited from the preceding section and transforms it through its own motion, inertia and pneumatic coupling.

The important point is that the system does not have a perfectly fixed operational zero.

A real loudspeaker driver is not perfectly symmetrical. Its positive and negative excursion limits are not necessarily identical:

$$
X_{\max+} \neq |X_{\max-}|
$$

At the same time, the instantaneous input signal continuously changes the direction and magnitude of diaphragm motion. Consequently, the effective zero around which the DZ pneumatic system operates becomes dynamic:

$$
0_{DZ}=0_{DZ}(t)
$$

Pneumatic inertia prevents the coupled air mass from following this changing zero instantaneously. The pneumatic state inherited from the preceding motion therefore continues to interact with the new diaphragm motion.

The +0 and −0 states are not two fixed physical zero points. They represent opposite turnover states of this dynamically moving reference.

The accordion illustration is not a literal mechanical model of DZ. It is intended to visualise how a distributed pneumatic mass can inherit, transform and transfer state.

### Dynamic Zero as a Consequence

Dynamic Zero is not an isolated mechanism within the system. It is a consequence of the system as a whole.

Its position reflects the combined interaction of the driver, pneumatic mass, DMI, compensator, input signal and inherited pneumatic state.

In this sense, Dynamic Zero can be viewed as an expression of the system's overall effectiveness: the better these elements work together, the more closely the resulting dynamic state follows the input.

> **DZ is not what makes the system work; DZ is what results from how the system works.**
