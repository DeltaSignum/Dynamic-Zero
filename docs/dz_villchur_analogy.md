## Conceptual Continuity: Villchur → Dynamic Zero

Dynamic Zero is not derived from acoustic suspension, and the two systems
should not be confused mechanically.

However, there is a useful conceptual relationship.

Villchur's acoustic-suspension principle demonstrated that the air inside a
loudspeaker enclosure does not have to be treated merely as an unavoidable
property of the box. It can be assigned a deliberate mechanical function.

In acoustic suspension:

**Air → restoring force (spring)**

In Dynamic Zero:

**Air → working fluid of a pneumatic system**

The distinction is important.

DZ does not simply replace one air spring with another. Diaphragm-induced
pressure and displacement are passed through a sequence of functional
pneumatic elements:

**Diaphragm → DMI → DM Impulser → DM Container → DM Delta Compensator → Atmosphere**

The DMI inevitably contains pneumatic compliance, just as any confined air
volume does. But in DZ this is only part of the process.

The proposed role of the DM Impulser is to convert the pressure/displacement
state produced by the diaphragm into accelerated air-mass motion and a
pneumatic impulse presented to the following stages.

The Container and Delta Compensator then participate in the evolution of
that pneumatic state before its interaction with the atmosphere.

Therefore, the conceptual relationship can be summarized simply:

> **Villchur: pressure becomes restoring force.**
>
> **DZ: pressure becomes pneumatic work.**

Or, even more simply:

> **Same air. Another job.**

This is a philosophical continuity, not a claim that Dynamic Zero is an
acoustic-suspension derivative.
